How a lesson works

Not a word list. A scene you can walk into.

Grammar is not why people freeze at a ticket window. They freeze because nobody told them what the moment sounds like. So each lesson is built backwards from the moment.

  • Read the scene first. Who you are talking to, what the place is like, and why the sentence is short or formal.
  • Hear it at two speeds. Every sentence plays in Korean at 1.0×, and at 0.8× when your ear needs the extra half-second.
  • Hide the romanization. One switch turns all the Latin letters off, so you read Hangul instead of translating it.
  • Say it out loud. The phrases are short on purpose — palace windows and pocha tables are loud places.
  • Finish with three questions. Instant answers, with the explanation that actually matters.

Who is behind the cup

A teacher, a girl with a coffee, and one very opinionated poodle.

Cup of Coffee Korean started on Instagram as one sentence a day — Daily KR sentence for you — because a sentence you can use by dinner is worth more than a page of vocabulary you cannot pronounce. Thousands of learners later, the sentences got longer, the culture notes got deeper, and this site is where they live in full.

The faces are the channel's own characters: the teacher you see in the videos, the girl who is always carrying a cup, and the poodle, who has no lines but strong opinions about 포차 snacks. Behind them is a real Korean teacher who writes and checks every sentence on this site.
